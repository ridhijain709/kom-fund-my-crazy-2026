01 The Core Problem: The Geographic Opportunity Divide

Across metropolitan India, over 12.4 million college students commute on public transit every single day. The average commuter spends 18.2 minutes standing at transit stops, waiting in silence or scrolling through social feeds.

Meanwhile, in corporate hubs like Gurugram, Bengaluru, and Mumbai, companies urgently need sharp, creative, practical thinkers. Yet traditional campus recruiting visits only a tiny fraction of top-tier colleges (the top 2%). A student studying in an ordinary college in Kanpur, Agra, or Varanasi might have remarkable problem-solving skills, but their resume is filtered out by automated screening software before any human recruiter ever reads it.

The problem is not that students lack talent. The problem is that they lack a physical bridge to show what they can actually do. Project KOM builds that bridge directly into their daily commute.

02 The Simple Solution: How KOM Works in Daily Life

KOM retrofits everyday public bus shelters and neighborhood library spaces with simple, clear display boards that feature real-world business and design puzzles from partner companies. Here is how a student experiences it:

STEP 1
Spot a Real-World Challenge at the Bus Stop
While waiting for their bus, a student looks up and sees a clear, intriguing 3-line problem posted by a real company. For example: "How can a local grocery network cut fresh food waste during peak summer heat?"
STEP 2
Scan the QR Code with Any Smartphone — No App Needed
The student scans a quick QR code. A lightweight webpage opens instantly in their phone browser. There is no app to download, no password to remember, and no sign-up fee. It works smoothly even on basic mobile connections.
STEP 3
Think, Type, or Speak on the Commute
Students answer three simple questions. They can type their response or record a 2-minute voice note while riding the bus, explaining their logic and practical ideas in their own natural words.
STEP 4
Helpful Socratic Guidance Powered by Google Gemini
Instead of grading like a strict exam, Gemini acts like a patient, encouraging mentor. It highlights strong reasoning and gently asks questions that help the student think deeper: "Great point on refrigeration! How would a small neighborhood store handle the electricity cost?"
STEP 5
Fair, Blind Evaluation Sent Directly to Hiring Teams
When a student submits a great solution, their answer is packaged into a clean dossier and sent directly to company hiring managers. The student's name, college name, and city are hidden so they are judged purely on merit, creativity, and practical logic. Top thinkers earn direct interview invites.

03 Why This Model Works: Mutual Value for Everyone

🎓 For Students
100% Free forever. Direct access to real industry puzzles, personalized thinking feedback, and interview opportunities without needing an elite college brand or privileged connections.
🏢 For Companies
High-quality talent at 92% lower cost. Replaces expensive campus recruitment tours (average ₹1,85,000 per hire) with pre-evaluated, genuinely creative thinkers for just ₹14,200 per placement.
🏛️ For Cities & Transit
Zero initial budget required. Turns ordinary bus shelters and public libraries into active community education hubs, boosting citizen social mobility without new civic taxes.

05 Realistic, Low-Risk Implementation Roadmap

Phase 01 • Months 1 to 3
Zero-Hardware Pilot
Launch immediately using existing computer systems in municipal libraries and college reading rooms. Validate student engagement and achieve the first 500 verified corporate placements.
Phase 02 • Months 4 to 6
High-Density Bus Corridor
Install 50 rugged, low-power electronic ink display units at student-heavy bus shelters along a single urban pilot corridor (e.g., Greater Noida to Delhi).
Phase 03 • Months 7 to 12
Regional Expansion
Scale to 250 transit shelters across major student commuting hubs across Uttar Pradesh and NCR, funded entirely by sustainable corporate talent subscriptions.

06 The Bigger Picture: Transforming Social Mobility

India does not have a shortage of smart, hard-working students. It has an infrastructure problem: the distance between where great minds grow up and where great companies hire is simply too wide.

By placing real-world problem-solving directly in the path of everyday commuters, Project KOM makes opportunity part of the city's physical landscape. It gives every student waiting for a bus the chance to prove their worth, sharpen their mind, and claim their rightful seat at the table.
